## What do we actually need?

Most small businesses do not need every security product on the market. They need to know what they depend on, which failures would hurt most, and which controls will reduce that risk without making daily work harder.

A one-time assessment answers that question. It reviews your systems, accounts, vendors, backups, policies, and current safeguards. The result is a prioritized plan that separates urgent work from useful improvements and items that can wait.

## What is a sensible budget?

A useful budget starts with business impact, existing tools, and available staff. It should not start with a vendor shopping list.

The assessment identifies where current spending is doing useful work, where a basic control is missing, and where complexity may cost more than it protects. Recommendations stay vendor-neutral so you can compare options without being pushed toward a particular product.

## What will our insurer ask?

Cyber insurance applications often ask about backups, multifactor authentication, endpoint protection, email security, access controls, training, and incident response. Renegade Penguin can help you understand those questions, gather evidence, and identify gaps. Your insurer and counsel remain the authorities on coverage and legal interpretation.

## Who do we call during an incident?

An incident is a bad time to exchange introductions. Planning establishes who makes decisions, who handles technical containment, and when to involve counsel, insurance, law enforcement, or outside specialists.
