## Do we know what is connected?

An asset inventory should show more than device names. It should identify purpose, location, ownership, software or firmware where known, network connections, vendor dependencies, and the operational consequence of failure. The inventory can then support maintenance, access decisions, monitoring, and incident planning.

## Are business and operational systems separated?

Segmentation limits how far a problem can travel between corporate IT, vendor access, engineering workstations, operational technology, and safety-sensitive systems. The right design depends on actual data flows and operating requirements. It may include network boundaries, controlled access paths, logging, and tested procedures for exceptional access.

## Can we make changes safely?

Operational technology cannot always be patched, restarted, or replaced on an ordinary IT schedule. Some systems have narrow maintenance windows, long service lives, specialized support agreements, or direct consequences when unavailable.

Security work should account for those facts. Changes need clear ownership, operational review, recovery steps, and testing proportionate to the risk. Where immediate remediation is unsafe, compensating controls can reduce exposure until a planned change is possible.

## Are we ready to respond without creating another problem?

Incident plans should define decision authority, communication paths, vendor contacts, evidence handling, and conditions for isolating or shutting down systems. Exercises can test those decisions before an actual event, including situations where normal communications or remote access are unavailable.
