Ohio organizations have a state-law reason to treat a written cybersecurity program as more than internal paperwork.

Ohio Revised Code sections 1354.02 and 1354.03 provide certain covered businesses with an affirmative defense in some lawsuits arising from a data breach when they create, maintain, and follow a written cybersecurity program that reasonably conforms to a recognized cybersecurity framework. The program must also be appropriate to factors such as the organization’s size, activities, information, available resources, and the cost and availability of security tools.

An affirmative defense is not immunity. It does not prevent a breach, stop a lawsuit from being filed, excuse every security failure, or decide the outcome of every claim. Eligibility depends on the facts, the type of claim, the information involved, and whether the organization satisfies the statutory requirements.

The practical work is familiar: choose an appropriate framework, document the program, assign responsibility, implement safeguards, retain evidence, review changes, and keep the program current. Renegade Penguin can help an organization assess its existing controls and build a program aligned to an applicable recognized framework. That technical work does not replace legal advice.
