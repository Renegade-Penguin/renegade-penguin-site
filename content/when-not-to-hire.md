A vCISO-style advisory retainer is useful when an organization needs ongoing security ownership, decision support, and a practical roadmap. It is the wrong purchase when the actual need is something else.

Do not hire Renegade Penguin for an advisory retainer if you need:

- A full-time executive managing staff, budgets, and daily operations
- A help desk or outsourced IT department
- Continuous monitoring, managed detection and response, or a staffed security operations center
- Emergency incident response already in progress
- A penetration test or other independent technical test
- Legal advice, privacy counsel, or an attestation
- Someone to approve a decision leadership has already made
- A document created only to satisfy a customer checkbox
- Assurance that an incident will not happen
- A specific product sold regardless of whether it fits

A defined project may be a better fit when the problem has a clear end point, such as hardening an environment, reviewing an architecture, improving incident readiness, or examining AI use and data flows. A full-time hire may be the better answer when the work requires daily authority and direct management. And a managed provider is usually the right choice when the need is continuous operations rather than advice.

The same honesty applies after an engagement starts. If the work no longer needs a retainer, the recommendation will be to reduce it, change the scope, or stop. Retainers are month to month with no lock-in.

## Vendor neutrality and conflicts

Renegade Penguin does not accept commissions for recommendations and has no reseller ties. Advice is based on the client’s needs, risks, constraints, and existing environment. There are no sacred cows, including tools the client already owns or tools that have worked well elsewhere.

Sometimes a project needs expertise outside one person’s lane. A network of specialists, including experts in telecom for call centers and penetration testing, can be brought in when appropriate. Their role, cost, and relationship to the work are disclosed before they are engaged.

Anything that could reasonably change the advice will be disclosed. If a conflict cannot be handled clearly, the work should go to someone else.
