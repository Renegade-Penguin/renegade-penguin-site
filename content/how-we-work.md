Renegade Penguin engagements start with a scoping call. The goal is to understand the problem, identify the people and systems involved, and decide whether the work is a good fit. You will leave with a proposed next step, not a surprise sales process.

## 1. Scoping call

**Typical duration:** 30 to 60 minutes.

We discuss your priorities, constraints, existing work, and desired outcome. If the engagement makes sense, you receive a written scope covering deliverables, responsibilities, timing, and fees. Work is done under a written agreement.

## 2. Assessment

**Typical duration:** one to three weeks, depending on access and scope.

The assessment establishes a useful baseline before recommendations or implementation begin. It may include document review, stakeholder interviews, architecture review, configuration review, or focused technical testing.

You receive:

- A concise statement of the current condition
- Confirmed findings separated from assumptions
- Immediate concerns that need attention
- Open questions and evidence gaps

An advisory retainer begins with this one-time assessment. That keeps the ongoing work tied to your actual environment instead of generic advice.

## 3. Plan

**Typical duration:** several business days after the assessment.

Findings are turned into a prioritized plan. Each recommendation includes the reason for the work, expected effort, dependencies, and a practical way to confirm completion.

You receive a roadmap that your team can use whether Renegade Penguin performs the work, advises your staff, or hands it to another provider.

## 4. Execution or advisory cadence

**Typical duration:** project work commonly runs two to eight weeks. Advisory retainers continue month to month.

Project work follows the agreed scope and produces documented changes, decisions, and validation results. Advisory work typically includes scheduled working sessions, review of active decisions, written follow-ups, and reasonable between-session questions.

Retainers are month to month with no long-term lock-in. Either side may end the engagement under the notice terms in the written agreement.

## 5. Review

At the end of a project, or at agreed checkpoints during a retainer, we review what changed, what remains open, and where risk or uncertainty still exists.

You receive updated findings, supporting notes, and recommended next actions. Technical work includes validation appropriate to the scope.

## 6. Offboarding

Access is removed or returned, open work is documented, and client data is deleted at the end of the engagement on request. You receive handover notes, final deliverables, and a list of unresolved items.

If the owner becomes unavailable, documented runbooks and current handover notes support continuity. A named backup contact from the owner’s expert network is identified for the engagement and may assist with coordination or handoff. No subcontractor performs client work without the client’s written consent.
