Community comes first. Much of the work is open source, and some help is provided at no cost to people and organizations without adequate resources.

## Support Where Resources Are Limited

I provide free help to smaller and minority communities that may not have access to cybersecurity and IT guidance. This work has included houses of worship, without naming the groups, faiths, clients, or places involved.

The goal is practical help that respects privacy, circumstances, and available resources.

## Safety and Privacy Education

I also support domestic violence organizations through education in OSINT, OPSEC, and threat modeling. This work helps people think more clearly about the information they expose, the ways they may be found or monitored, and the risks involved in getting away from abusive partners and stalking.

The subject requires care. Advice must account for the person’s situation, the systems around them, and the possibility that an abusive person may already have access to devices, accounts, or personal information.

## Open Work

Open source work allows useful defensive knowledge to reach people beyond a single engagement. Sharing practical tools and methods strengthens the wider community while giving others the ability to examine, test, and improve the work.

I volunteer with Call Center Village, a hands-on village for voice social engineering.
