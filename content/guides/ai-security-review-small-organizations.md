An AI security review is a fixed-scope examination of how an organization actually uses AI, including use that never went through formal approval. A small organization may need one because employees can adopt AI services quickly, while sensitive-data exposure, access, retention, and vendor questions remain unresolved.

## The review starts with actual use

A policy cannot describe the risk if nobody knows which tools people use. The review therefore looks beyond the approved software list and builds a shadow AI inventory: AI services, embedded assistants, browser tools, meeting features, development tools, and other uses that may have entered normal work informally.

The purpose is not to punish employees for finding useful tools. It is to understand what work is being performed, what information enters each service, who can access it, and which assumptions have never been checked.

That inventory gives leadership something concrete to evaluate. “The organization uses AI” is too vague to support a decision. A named tool, a specific workflow, and a known data flow can be reviewed.

## What the review covers

A Renegade Penguin AI security review examines:

- Acceptable-use expectations for employees and contractors
- A shadow AI inventory, including unapproved or overlooked use
- Data flows and possible sensitive-data exposure
- User access, administrative access, and account ownership
- Retention and model-training questions
- Vendor security claims and relevant contract questions
- Practical controls for AI use the organization chooses to approve

Vendor language deserves careful review. Claims about privacy, training, deletion, isolation, or enterprise controls may apply only to certain plans, settings, or contract terms. The review separates what the vendor states from what the organization has actually configured or agreed to.

The review can identify contract questions, but it is not legal advice. Contract negotiation is scoped separately.

## What the organization receives

The output should help leadership make decisions, not merely describe AI risk. Findings are organized around actual uses, exposed information, unresolved questions, and controls that fit approved workflows.

A practical recommendation might restrict certain information, require organization-managed accounts, assign an owner, change a setting, or pause a use until a vendor answers a specific question. The right control depends on how the tool is used and what information reaches it.

## What the review does not promise

An AI security review does not declare a tool safe, establish legal compliance, or control whether employees follow policy. It gives the organization a defensible view of current use and a practical basis for deciding what to allow, restrict, change, or investigate further.

## Questions

### Is this only for organizations with an approved AI program?

No. Unapproved use is part of the review. In many cases, the first useful step is discovering which AI services have already entered daily work.

### Does the review include penetration testing?

Not by default. Broader technical testing is scoped separately when the identified systems, risks, and access make it appropriate.
